# Intelligent Art-Direction Engine

This engine converts taste, references, websites, product facts, and creative ambition into a defensible poster direction. It protects exploration early, then converges using evidence. The scores support judgment; they do not replace it.

## Governing principles

1. **Product truth outranks style.** Start from what the product is, does, feels like, or makes possible.
2. **References are evidence, not templates.** Extract decisions from them; do not inherit their complete visual identity.
3. **Creativity is relevant surprise.** Bend one rule in a way that reveals the product. Random weirdness is not a concept.
4. **One dominant idea wins.** Secondary information may deepen it but must not create another poster inside the poster.
5. **Every decision is traceable.** Camera, color, type, material, and composition must connect to product, audience, message, or reference evidence.
6. **Hard gates precede taste scores.** False claims, uncertain rights, broken logos, inaccurate packs, unreadable copy, and obvious AI slop cannot be averaged away by high visual scores.

## Art-direction DNA

Translate every brief into this compact decision object:

| DNA field | Question | Output |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Research question | What are we trying to learn from the sources? | One job-linked question |
| Product truth | What is undeniably specific to this product? | One factual sentence |
| Human meaning | What desire, ritual, emotion, or identity does it serve? | One human sentence |
| Communication job | What must the poster make the viewer know, feel, or do? | One action sentence |
| Audience context | Where and how will the audience encounter it? | Audience + channel |
| Memory object | What single object, action, or silhouette should remain in memory? | Named hero/carrier |
| Controlled contradiction | What expected rule can be bent to create surprise? | One rule and one reason |
| Concept operator | What perceptual action makes the message visible? | One primary operator or `direct` |
| Ambition level | How far should the visual transformation be pushed? | Refined, expressive, carrier, or world-building |
| Attention path | What is read first, second, and last? | Hero → message → proof/CTA sequence |
| Proof detail | What visible detail proves the promise? | Ingredient, texture, action, label, result, or ritual |
| Story scale | Is this a symbol, scene, or ritual? | One scale |
| Reveal level | Full, hinted, or suspenseful? | Reveal strategy |
| Realism mode | Studio, lifestyle, print, collage, illustration, or commercial surrealism? | One primary mode |
| Camera logic | Which viewpoint makes the idea strongest? | Viewpoint + lens feel |
| Color logic | What relationship carries the emotion and hierarchy? | Base + hero + counterpoint |
| Type logic | What display, utility, script, and brand roles are needed? | Type-role system |
| Material logic | What surfaces and imperfections make the world believable? | Material/texture list |
| Treatment stack | Which ordered processes create the chosen world, and why? | Transform → palette → medium → energy → surface, using only needed jobs |
| Recognition anchor | What remains clean enough to guarantee recognition? | Pack, face, headline, proof, or CTA |
| Rights boundary | What may be reused, transformed, or treated only as reference? | Provenance statement |

If product truth, human meaning, communication job, audience context, or memory object remains vague, pause visual production and strengthen the brief. A research question is required when references or external sources materially shape the direction.

### Evidence labels

Mark important inputs as:

- **`V` — Verified:** supplied by the user, visible in an authorized asset, or confirmed by an authoritative source.
- **`I` — Inferred:** a reasonable visual or audience interpretation that still needs judgment.
- **`A` — Assumed:** used temporarily to keep work moving.

Product claims, prices, dates, legal copy, logo/packaging fidelity, and rights status may not remain `A` when a direction is locked. A strong visual score does not compensate for weak evidence.

## Project state model

| State | Meaning | Required artifact | Exit condition |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `INTAKE` | Inputs collected | Brief and asset inventory | Objective, format, and rights assumptions are known |
| `EVIDENCE_MAPPED` | Sources understood | Reference cards/source brief | Product truth and reusable principles are named |
| `TERRITORIES` | Creative possibilities separated | Up to three concept territories | Each territory has a different memory object or story mechanism |
| `DIRECTION_LOCKED` | One route chosen | Completed DNA + score | Hard gates pass and selected territory scores at least 80/100 |
| `BUILT` | Hero and layout assembled | Working poster | Copy, product, type, and image are integrated |
| `AUDITED` | Craft and slop review complete | Gate results | No hard fail remains; crop/thumbnail/full-size checks pass |
| `DELIVERED` | Final package handed off | Final files + rationale | Dimensions, versions, rights note, and assumptions are included |

Do not skip from intake directly to build unless the user supplies a fully locked concept and complete assets. Even then, capture the DNA briefly so later decisions remain coherent.

## Reference influence map

For each reference, mark its contribution from 0–3:

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Concept/story | None | General mood | Related mechanism | Strong structural influence |
| Composition | None | Loose balance | Similar arrangement family | Near-direct layout influence |
| Camera | None | Similar distance | Similar viewpoint | Nearly identical shot logic |
| Color | None | One shared hue | Similar relationship | Near-identical palette |
| Typography | None | Similar role | Similar category/hierarchy | Near-identical treatment |
| Material/texture | None | General finish | Similar process | Near-identical surface system |

This is a design-distance warning, not legal clearance. If one reference scores `3` in four or more dimensions, the direction is too dependent on it. Rebuild the concept from product truth or distribute influence: one reference for structure, another for color, another for material, while the product supplies the idea and copy.

## Creative territory generator

When the brief is open, create no more than three routes:

1. **Direct truth:** Make the product, ingredient, texture, or benefit impossible to miss.
2. **Human story:** Show the person, ritual, place, or cultural behavior around the product.
3. **Controlled surprise:** Use a product-specific metaphor or altered-physics move that remains believable enough to sell.

Each route must state:

```text
Territory name:
Concept sentence:
Memory object:
Product proof:
Primary concept operator:
Ambition level:
Attention path:
Treatment stack and order:
Recognition anchor:
Dominant gesture:
Camera/viewpoint:
Color relationship:
Type voice:
Why this audience will care:
Reference lineage:
Copyright-distance risk:
Production risk:
```

If two territories differ only in palette or font, they are variants, not different ideas.

## Evidence-weighted decision score

Score each territory from 1–5 in every category. Calculate `total = Σ(score ÷ 5 × weight)` for a maximum of 100.

| Category | Weight | What earns a high score |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Product truth | 20 | The idea could only belong to this product or story |
| Concept specificity | 15 | Clear, concrete, explainable in one sentence |
| Memorability | 15 | One image/action survives at thumbnail size and after viewing |
| Composition and hierarchy | 10 | First, second, and third reads are deliberate |
| Color logic | 10 | Palette supports emotion, product, and hierarchy |
| Typography and language | 10 | Type roles are purposeful, accurate, and script-aware |
| Camera, material, and physics | 10 | Viewpoint and realism make the idea convincing |
| Originality and rights distance | 5 | Influence is traceable without becoming a clone |
| Production feasibility | 5 | Can be executed at the required quality, ratio, and deadline |

Interpretation:

- **Below 70:** reject; the route is not ready.
- **70–79:** revise the weakest high-weight category.
- **80–89:** direction may be locked after hard gates pass.
- **90–100:** exceptional candidate; still subject to fidelity, rights, and anti-slop gates.

### Hard gates

Any one of these blocks approval regardless of score:

- Unverified or fabricated product claim.
- Unclear permission for a source image intended for direct reuse.
- Recreated, distorted, or hallucinated logo/packaging.
- Critical copy or multilingual text cannot be typeset accurately.
- The same concept/layout could advertise an unrelated product unchanged.
- Reference cloning across concept, composition, palette, typography, and copy.
- Generic AI filler is carrying the composition.
- The visual operator is removable without weakening the message, or several unrelated operators are competing.

## Fair selection rules

“Fair” means choosing against the brief and evidence rather than personal attachment:

- Score before discussing favorite aesthetics.
- Write one sentence of evidence for every score of 4 or 5.
- Do not penalize simplicity when one object communicates the message clearly.
- Do not reward complexity merely because it took more work.
- Keep uncertainty visible; mark assumptions rather than presenting them as facts.
- In a tie, choose the route with stronger product truth, clearer one-second recognition, safer rights distance, and fewer fragile production dependencies—in that order.

## Route selector

| Situation | Preferred starting route | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| New or unfamiliar product | Label-forward studio or direct truth | Hiding the pack before recognition is established |
| Ingredient/flavor is the main differentiator | Ingredient architecture or material world | Generic fruit scattered around the product |
| Premium finish or physical quality matters | Macro, controlled light, sparse typography | Busy collage that hides surfaces |
| Event, offer, or local activation | Information-first scene with strong hero and CTA | Art poster with missing date, place, or action |
| Culture, heritage, or place matters | Specific material/ritual language | Generic “ethnic” patterns or costume cues |
| Familiar brand needs renewed attention | Human story or controlled surprise | Repeating the standard packshot unchanged |
| Product benefit is invisible | Metaphor with visible proof | Abstract symbols that require explanation |
| Multiple aspect ratios are required | Composition with a central hero and movable information zones | Edge-dependent idea that collapses when cropped |

## 100% potential rule

Use maximum creative freedom only where evidence is strong. Preserve product, claim, language, and rights fidelity; concentrate experimentation in one or two chosen dimensions—such as scale, physics, camera, material, or typographic integration. This creates a bold result without making every layer compete.

## Learning loop

After delivery, record:

- What the viewer understood first.
- Which detail made the product memorable.
- What failed at thumbnail, full size, or alternate crop.
- Which reference principle transferred successfully.
- Which assumption was wrong.

Update the system only when a pattern repeats across projects. Do not turn one subjective reaction into a universal rule.
